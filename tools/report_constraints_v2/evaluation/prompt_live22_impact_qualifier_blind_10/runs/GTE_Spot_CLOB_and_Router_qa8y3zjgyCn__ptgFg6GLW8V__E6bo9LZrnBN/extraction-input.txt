Zero-Cost Order Spam Forces `POST_ONLY` Users to Pay Higher Taker Fees

## Finding description and impact

An attacker can systematically block `POST_ONLY` orders from other traders by placing orders with very short expiration times, forcing victims to use GTC orders that become takers and pay higher fees. This attack is economically viable because order placement is free and fees only apply on matching.

The CLOB protocol allows orders to be placed with expiration timestamps. When an order expires, it is removed from the book and the maker receives a full refund of their tokens without any fees. This creates a vulnerability where an attacker can place orders with very short expiration times (e.g., 1 second) to temporarily block `POST_ONLY` orders from other traders.

### Key Vulnerability Factors

1. **Free Order Placement**: Submitting an order to the book is completely free - no fees are charged for order placement
2. **Fees Only on Matching**: Trading fees (maker/taker fees) are only applied when orders are actually matched and executed
3. **POST_ONLY Guarantee**: The `POST_ONLY` order type is specifically designed to guarantee that the user will be a **maker** (not a taker), ensuring they pay the lower maker fees
4. **Fee Differential**: Taker fees (0.05% in tests) are significantly higher than maker fees (0.03%).

### Attack Mechanism

The attack works as follows:

1. **Attacker places expiring orders**: The attacker places orders at competitive prices with `cancelTimestamp = block.timestamp + 1`, flooding the order book with orders strategically placed around the spot price (here 80-90% of trading volume occurs)
2. **POST_ONLY orders are blocked**: When victims try to place `POST_ONLY` orders at the same price, they are rejected because `getBestAskPrice() <= newOrder.price` or `getBestBidPrice() >= newOrder.price`
3. **Victims forced to be takers**: Victims must use GTC orders instead, which immediately match and become takers, violating the POST_ONLY guarantee
4. **Fee differential exploited**: Takers pay more fees 

The key vulnerability is in the `POST_ONLY` validation logic:


When expiring orders are present in the book, they temporarily set the best bid/ask prices, blocking legitimate `POST_ONLY` orders even though the expiring orders will be removed shortly.

###  Impact Explanation

**Medium Impact**: This attack can force traders to pay higher fees (0.05% vs 0.03%) and disrupt normal market making activities. The attack violates the fundamental guarantee of `POST_ONLY` orders - that users will be makers and pay lower fees. While the direct loss is limited to fee differentials, it creates an unfair advantage for attackers and can significantly impact trading costs for legitimate market makers who rely on `POST_ONLY` orders to ensure maker status.

###  Likelihood Explanation

**High Likelihood**: This attack requires minimal capital (only the tokens needed for the expiring orders, which are fully refunded) and can be executed by any user. The fact that order placement is free and fees only apply on matching makes this attack particularly easy to execute repeatedly.
