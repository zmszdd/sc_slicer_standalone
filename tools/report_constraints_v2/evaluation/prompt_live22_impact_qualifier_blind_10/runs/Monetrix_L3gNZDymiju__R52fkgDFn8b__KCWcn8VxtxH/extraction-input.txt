Premature 0x811 supplied-slot registration can brick Accountant backing reads

### Summary

`MonetrixVault.executeHedge()` and `MonetrixVault.supplyToBlp()` optimistically register supplied-balance slots in `MonetrixAccountant` immediately after sending CoreWriter actions. The registration is not confirmed against HyperCore. If the CoreWriter action is accepted locally but the corresponding 0x811 supplied slot does not exist or is not yet active, the Accountant records a slot that cannot be read.

`MonetrixAccountant.totalBackingSigned()` fail-closes on every registered 0x811 slot. One premature or inactive supplied-slot entry therefore makes aggregate backing reads revert. The revert propagates to `surplus()`, `distributableSurplus()`, settlement, and yield/bridge accounting flows that depend on those reads.

### Root cause



- `executeHedge()` sends the hedge actions and then immediately calls `notifyVaultSupply()` when `pmEnabled` is true.
- `supplyToBlp()` sends the supply action and then immediately calls `notifyVaultSupply()`.
- `notifyVaultSupply()` only appends the slot to the Accountant registry; it does not confirm that 0x811 can read that slot successfully.
- Later, `totalBackingSigned()` iterates the registered supplied slots and calls strict 0x811 readers. A failed 0x811 read reverts the entire backing calculation.

### Impact

This is a protocol availability/accounting failure, not direct fund theft. Once a bad supplied slot is registered, core accounting reads revert until the registry is manually cleaned up. Affected paths include:

- `totalBackingSigned()`
- `surplus()`
- `distributableSurplus()`
- `settleDailyPnL()`
- Vault reads that depend on Accountant surplus, including yield shortfall calculations

The issue is Medium severity because protocol accounting and settlement availability can be impacted through a plausible normal Operator/CoreWriter flow, but assets are not directly stolen by the demonstrated path.
