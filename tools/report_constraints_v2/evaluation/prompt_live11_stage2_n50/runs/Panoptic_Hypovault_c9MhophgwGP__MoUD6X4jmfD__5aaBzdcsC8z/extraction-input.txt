H-19: Users Can Merge Profitable and Losing Positions via `transfer` to Systematically Evade Performance Fees

## Finding description and impact

### Description

The `HypoVault` contract implements an "Average Cost Basis" accounting model to calculate performance fees on profitable withdrawals. A user's total cost basis for all their shares is stored in a single `userBasis` variable. When shares are transferred from one user to another, the `_transferBasis` internal function is triggered. This function calculates the proportional basis of the transferred shares and adds it to the recipient's `userBasis`.

The root cause of the vulnerability lies in this `_transferBasis` function's "indiscriminate merging" logic:


The line `userBasis[to] += basisToTransfer;` simply adds the incoming basis to the recipient's existing basis. It does not distinguish between a recipient's pre-existing profitable position (where `market value > basis`) and the incoming position's financial history.

This allows for a straightforward economic exploit. A user (Alice) who has a highly profitable position can collude with another user (Bob) who has a position at a loss. Bob can transfer his shares to Alice. His high-cost basis, representing a loss, is then merged into Alice's low-cost basis. This effectively "mixes" the profitable and losing positions into a single, new average cost basis in Alice's account.

This new, artificially inflated average cost basis can be manipulated to be equal to or greater than the current market value of the shares, making the calculated profit zero or negative upon withdrawal. As a result, the performance fee, which is a core income source for the protocol, is completely eliminated.

### Impact

This is a high-risk economic vulnerability with severe consequences for the protocol's financial health and fairness:

1.  **Systematic Evasion of Core Protocol Revenue**: It allows any two or more colluding users to systematically and completely evade performance fees. This creates a public, easily replicable strategy that would, if widely adopted, reduce the protocol's fee income to near zero, undermining its economic model.

2.  **Creation of a "Loss Trading" Market**: The vulnerability inadvertently creates a secondary market for "tax-loss harvesting". Users with losing positions can "sell" their high-cost basis to profitable users. This is an unintended and unhealthy market dynamic that gamifies the fee structure.

3.  **Fundamental Unfairness**: It creates a two-tiered system. Sophisticated users誰 are aware of this mechanism can avoid fees, while regular, long-term holders who do not engage in such strategies will bear the full cost. This undermines the principle of a fair and transparent platform.

4.  **Flawed Economic Model**: The combination of transferable shares (ERC20) and a simple average cost basis accounting system is fundamentally flawed. It demonstrates that the protocol cannot simultaneously support both features securely, hvilket is a critical design flaw.
