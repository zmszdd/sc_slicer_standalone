Force Exercise Fee Evasion via Atomic Spot Price Manipulation

## Finding description and impact

### Description
The `RiskEngine.exerciseCost` function is responsible for calculating the fee that a "Force Exercisor" (typically an option seller) must pay to an "Exercisee" (an option buyer) when forcefully closing their position. 

The protocol implements a tiered fee structure to protect active positions:
1.  **In-Range Positions:** If a position is currently "In-Range" (the underlying asset price is close to the strike price), the fee is high (`FORCE_EXERCISE_COST` ≈ 1.024% of notional). This discourages sellers from forcefully closing active positions unless absolutely necessary.
2.  **Out-of-Range Positions:** If a position is "Out-of-Range", the fee is nominal (`ONE_BPS` ≈ 0.01% of notional). This incentivizes the cleanup of stale liquidity.

The vulnerability exists in how the protocol determines if a position is "In-Range". The `exerciseCost` function iterates through the position's legs and checks the **`currentTick`** (the instantaneous spot price) against the strike range:


Because `currentTick` reflects the instantaneous spot price of the Uniswap pool, it can be manipulated atomically within a transaction via a flash swap.

An attacker (e.g., an option seller blocked by a utilized pool) can perform the following sandwich attack in a single transaction:
1.  **Manipulate Spot Price:** Execute a swap on the Uniswap pool to push the `currentTick` just outside the range of the victim's position. This deviation can be small enough to stay within the protocol's "Safe Mode" thresholds (`MAX_TICKS_DELTA`).
2.  **Force Exercise:** Call `PanopticPool.forceExercise()`. The `RiskEngine` sees the `currentTick` is outside the range, sets `hasLegsInRange` to `false`, and charges the attacker the minimal `1 bps` fee instead of the intended `102 bps`.
3.  **Reverse Swap:** Swap back to restore the price, recovering the capital used in step 1 (minus swap fees).

### Impact
*   **Theft of Yield:** Option buyers (Exercisees) are deprived of the protection premium (~1% of position value) they are economically entitled to.
*   **Broken Protocol Invariant:** The mechanism designed to protect in-the-money/active positions is bypassed, allowing sellers to exit obligations cheaply at the expense of buyers.
*   **Economic Damage:** On a standard 5bps fee tier pool, the cost to shift the tick momentarily is negligible compared to the 1% fee savings. This makes the attack highly profitable and practically guaranteed to occur whenever liquidity utilization is high.
