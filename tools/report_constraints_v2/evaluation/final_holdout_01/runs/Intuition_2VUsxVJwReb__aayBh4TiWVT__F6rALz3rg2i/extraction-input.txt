## Finding description and impact
The protocol intends for users to only claim rewards for the previous epoch (currentEpoch  1), proportional to their locked deposits during that timeframe. However, a flaw in the balance snapshot mechanism allows an attacker to deposit at the exact boundary of an epoch, instantly claim rewards, and severely dilute honest users
The bug stems from how TrustBonding evaluates historical balances. When a user calls claimRewards(), the contract fetches their eligible rewards via `_userEligibleRewardsForEpoch()`, which in turn queries userBondedBalanceAtEpochEnd().

As shown in the contract logic, `userBondedBalanceAtEpochEnd` evaluates the user's balance at exactly `_epochTimestampEnd(epoch)`.

The protocol fails to enforcecontinuous time-weighted lock across the entire epoch. Because the exact end timestamp of the previous epoch perfectly aligns with the start of the new epoch, an attacker can execute a deposit transaction right before the epoch concludes.
## Impact
- malicious user can wait until the epoch ends, deposit capital with zero time-risk, and extract a massive share of the rewards, directly diluting users who held liquidity risk for the entire epoch.
