Integer Truncation Leads to Incorrect Liquidity Calculation in `launchFor()`

## Finding description and impact

The `launchFor()` function in `Bonding.sol` calculates the amount of `assetToken` (`liquidity`) required to pair with the newly minted token supply for initial liquidity provision using the following formula:


Where `K` is a contract constant (3×1012), `assetRate` is a value set during initialization, and `supply` is the total supply of the new token.

The root cause of this finding is the reliance on standard **integer division** in Solidity (`/`). Integer division truncates any remainder, effectively rounding down to the nearest whole number. While the formula includes scaling factors (`10000`, `1 ether`) to maintain precision, the structure of the calculation involves multiple division steps where truncation can occur.

Specifically, truncation can happen in the first division `(K * 10000) / assetRate` and in the divisions within the second line `(((k * 10000 ether) / supply) * 1 ether) / 10000`. If the intermediate numerator is not perfectly divisible by the denominator at any of these steps, the fractional part is lost.

-   The calculated `liquidity` amount will deviate from the mathematically precise value intended by the bonding curve formula. This leads to:
    
    -   **Incorrect Initial Price:** The ratio of `supply` to `liquidity` determines the initial price of the new token on the decentralized exchange pair. An incorrect `liquidity` value means the token is listed at a price different from what the bonding curve intends.
        
    -   **Economic Invariant Breach:** Bonding curves are designed to maintain specific mathematical relationships between token supply, price, and reserves. Truncation breaks this invariant, potentially affecting the fairness and predictability of the bonding mechanism for early participants and liquidity providers.
        
    -   **Unexpected Slippage:** Subsequent trades interacting with a pool initialized with an incorrect liquidity ratio may experience unexpected slippage compared to the theoretical bonding curve price.
