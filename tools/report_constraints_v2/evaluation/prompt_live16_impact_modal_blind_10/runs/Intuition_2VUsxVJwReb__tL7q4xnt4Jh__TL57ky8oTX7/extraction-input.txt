`AtomWallet.owner()` Makes an External Call During ERC-4337 Validation Phase, Violating ERC-7562 and Breaking UserOp Submission for All Unclaimed Wallets

## Finding Description and Impact

### Root Cause

`AtomWallet.owner()` has two branches depending on whether the wallet has been claimed:


When `isClaimed == false`, ownership is delegated to `multiVault.getAtomWarden()` — an external call to the `MultiVault` contract. `owner()` is invoked from `_validateSignature` at the point where the recovered signer is compared against the expected owner:


`_validateSignature` is the implementation of the ERC-4337 validation hook and is called by `validateUserOp` during the **validation phase**.

### ERC-7562 Violation — Rules STO-021 / STO-022

Reference: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7562

ERC-7562 governs what storage an account's validation phase may access. Rules **[STO-021]** and **[STO-022]** permit reading storage in an external contract only when the accessed slot is **associated** with the account. A storage slot in contract X is associated with account A if and only if:

1. The slot's **value equals A** (e.g. a mapping keyed by the wallet address), or
2. The slot was **derived as** `keccak(A || x) + n` (standard Solidity mapping/array at a key of A).

`multiVault.getAtomWarden()` reads `walletConfig.atomWarden` from `MultiVault`'s storage. That slot:
- Holds the warden address, **not** the `AtomWallet`'s address
- Was **not** derived from `keccak(atomWalletAddress || ...)` — it is a plain struct field

It therefore does not meet either condition: the accessed storage is **not associated** with the calling `AtomWallet`. Accessing it from within `validateUserOp` violates `STO-021`/`STO-022`. A compliant bundler simulating the UserOp detects the non-associated external storage read and **rejects the UserOp**. This is not a configuration issue — it is a fundamental incompatibility between the `owner()` implementation and the ERC-7562 storage access rules.

### Scope of Impact

Every `AtomWallet` begins life unclaimed (`isClaimed == false`), with `ATOM_WARDEN` as its effective owner via the dynamic `owner()` lookup. The wallet can only be claimed once the user calls `acceptOwnership()` after a two-step transfer. Until that happens:

- Every UserOp submitted for the wallet will be **rejected at simulation** by any ERC-7562-compliant bundler (e.g., Pimlico, Alchemy, Biconomy, Stackup, the canonical eth-infinitism bundler).
- The wallet cannot be used as an ERC-4337 smart account until claimed.
- The `ATOM_WARDEN` — the party responsible for acting on behalf of unclaimed atoms — is also blocked from submitting UserOps on those atoms via standard bundling infrastructure.

Given that unclaimed wallets are the initial state for **all** atoms created in the system, this effectively disables ERC-4337 functionality for every newly created atom.
