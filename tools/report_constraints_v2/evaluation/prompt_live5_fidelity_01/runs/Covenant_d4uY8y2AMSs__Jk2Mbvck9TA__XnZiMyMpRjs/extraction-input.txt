Mint-cap bypass via pre-state supply check allows one-shot large growth and deposit DoS (ETWAP lag)

## Finding description and impact

`LatentSwapLogic._checkMintCap` gates growth using the **pre-action** `marketBaseTokenSupply`. When the market is “small” (i.e., `marketBaseTokenSupply <= 2^noCapLimit`), the small-market branch permits any `mintAmount <= 2^96` (Q96) even if that amount would make the market “large” right after the action. The cap is **not re-evaluated** against the post-mint supply. `Covenant.mint` calls `LEX.mint(...)` passing `localBaseSupply` (cached pre-state). LEX calls `_checkMintCap` against that value. Only after the LEX call returns does Covenant update `ms.baseSupply`. The same ordering applies to BASE-exact-in swaps that mint under the hood.


1. **One-shot large growth bypass.** An attacker starts with a tiny market (≤ `2^noCapLimit`; ≈ ~1 BASE for 18-decimals with `noCapLimit = 60`) and mints **100e18** BASE. This passes the *small-market* branch (≤ 2^96), bypassing what the *large-market* ETWAP growth bound would have rejected.
2. **Deposit DoS / bricking via ETWAP lag.** Immediately after the oversized mint, subsequent **legitimate small mints** (e.g., 1e18) revert with `E_LEX_MintCapExceeded` because ETWAP still reflects the prior tiny market. Deposits are time-gated until ETWAP catches up, creating a practical DoS on adding collateral.

This requires no privileged roles, no oracle manipulation, and no governance actions, only being early to mint when the market is small. Consequently, any newly created market with small initial supply is susceptible; early actors can grow supply in one jump and temporarily block others from depositing, impacting market bootstrapping.
