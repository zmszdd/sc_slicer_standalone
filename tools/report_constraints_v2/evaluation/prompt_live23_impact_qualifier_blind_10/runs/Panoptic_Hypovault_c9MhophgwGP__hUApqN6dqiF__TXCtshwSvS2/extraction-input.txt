Missing Slippage Protection in `executeDeposit` Function Allows MEV Attacks and Unfair Exchange Rates

## Description

The `executeDeposit` function in the HypoVault contract lacks slippage protection, allowing users to receive fewer shares than expected for their deposited assets. This vulnerability stems from the two-phase deposit system where users have no control over the exchange rate determination.

**File:** `src/HypoVault.sol`  

and the execute function: 


**Related Function:** `fulfillDeposits` 


## Impact

MEV bots can sandwich the `fulfillDeposits()` call to manipulate the exchange rate. Users have **zero control** over when their deposits are fulfilled

- The exchange rate is determined entirely by the manager's timing of `fulfillDeposits()`
- Users may receive significantly fewer shares than expected due to:
  - Market volatility between deposit request and fulfillment
  - Manager's strategic timing of fulfillment
  - NAV calculation manipulation

## Attack Scenarios

1. User calls `requestDeposit(1000 USDC)`
2. MEV bot front-runs with large deposit to inflate NAV
3. Manager calls `fulfillDeposits()` with inflated NAV
4. User's `executeDeposit()` receives fewer shares due to inflated exchange rate
5. MEV bot back-runs with withdrawal to profit
