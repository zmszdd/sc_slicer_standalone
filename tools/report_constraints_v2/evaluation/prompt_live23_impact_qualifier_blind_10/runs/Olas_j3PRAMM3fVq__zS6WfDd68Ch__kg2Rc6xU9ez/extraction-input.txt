Missing TWAP Validation for Inactive and New Pools Enables Forced Capital Inefficiency

## Summary

The `checkPoolAndGetCenterPrice` function in `LiquidityManagerCore` is designed to protect liquidity operations from price manipulation by validating the current pool price against a 30-minute Time-Weighted Average Price (TWAP). However, the implementation contains a flaw: it returns the unvalidated spot price from `slot0` in two scenarios: (1) when the most recent observation is older than 30 minutes (inactive pools), and (2) when the TWAP query fails due to insufficient observation history (new pools). This creates systematic vulnerability windows where attackers can front-run owner-initiated liquidity operations by manipulating the spot price used to calculate tick ranges for new positions, resulting in positions minted entirely out-of-range that earn zero fees and require costly rebalancing operations.

## Impact

This creates a **Price Manipulation Attack Vector** enabling **Forced Capital Inefficiency**:

1. **Attack Likelihood - HIGH**: Any actor can monitor the mempool for owner transactions targeting vulnerable pools. The attack requires only flash loan capital for price manipulation (fully reversible via back-run) and gas costs. No privileged roles required. Vulnerable pools include all newly deployed Uniswap V3 pools until sufficient observation history accumulates and any pools with inactive trading where the most recent observation exceeds 30 minutes old.

2. **Attack Mechanism**: Attacker front-runs the owner's transaction with a large swap manipulating the spot price. When the owner's transaction executes, `checkPoolAndGetCenterPrice` returns the manipulated price because TWAP validation is bypassed. The `_calculateTicksAndMintPosition` function calculates `centerTick = TickMath.getTickAtSqrtRatio(sqrtP)` using the manipulated price, then derives `ticks[0] = centerTick + tickShifts[0]` and `ticks[1] = centerTick + tickShifts[1]`. When the attacker back-runs to restore the true price, the newly minted position is centered around the manipulated price and becomes entirely out-of-range at the true market price.

3. **Economic Impact**: The attack forces protocol funds into Uniswap V3 positions that are out-of-range and earn zero fees. While the funds remain in protocol-controlled positions and are not directly stolen, the protocol suffers from: (a) complete loss of fee-earning opportunity while the position remains out-of-range, (b) gas costs required to rebalance the position back into productive range, (c) potential impermanent loss realization when closing or rebalancing the position depending on price movement between position creation and correction, and (d) opportunity cost from capital being locked in non-productive positions rather than earning yield.

4. **Systemic Risk**: Every new pool deployment creates a vulnerability window until sufficient observations accumulate. Inactive pools with sporadic trading remain perpetually vulnerable until trading activity resumes. Cross-chain deployments targeting fresh Uniswap V3 pools expose initial capital deployment to this attack. Multiple sequential operations on different vulnerable pools compound the economic damage.

5. **Severity Classification**: This vulnerability does not constitute direct theft or permanent fund lockout as the funds remain in protocol-controlled Uniswap V3 positions accessible via `decreaseLiquidity` or `changeRanges` functions. However, it represents a **High Severity** issue due to guaranteed capital inefficiency, assured opportunity cost from zero fee earnings, required operational overhead to detect and correct mispositioned liquidity, and cumulative economic impact when multiple positions are affected or positions remain uncorrected.
