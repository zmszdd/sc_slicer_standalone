Settlement vault withdraw/redeem lack caller auth — anyone can drain self‑allowed users

## Summary
`WERC7575Vault.withdraw` and `redeem` never check that `msg.sender` is the owner (or an approved delegate). They only spend the owner→owner self‑allowance required for normal withdrawals. Once a validator issues that self‑allowance permit, any address can call withdraw/redeem for that owner, burn the owner’s shares, and receive the underlying assets to an arbitrary receiver.

## Impact
Full theft of a user’s settlement assets after they obtain the required self‑allowance permit. Attacker burns the victim’s shares and transfers all assets to themselves; the victim is left with zero shares and zero assets.

## Root Cause
- `_withdraw` is invoked by both `withdraw` and `redeem` and only calls `_shareToken.spendSelfAllowance(owner, shares)` without verifying `msg.sender == owner` or spending an allowance for the caller. Permalinks: [withdraw helper](https://github.com/code-423n4/2025-11-sukukfi/blob/main/src/WERC7575Vault.sol#L397-L410), [withdraw entrypoint](https://github.com/code-423n4/2025-11-sukukfi/blob/main/src/WERC7575Vault.sol#L434-L437), [redeem entrypoint](https://github.com/code-423n4/2025-11-sukukfi/blob/main/src/WERC7575Vault.sol#L464-L467).
- NatSpec comments claim “msg.sender must be owner OR have allowance” (e.g., lines 414‑452) but no such check exists in the code.
