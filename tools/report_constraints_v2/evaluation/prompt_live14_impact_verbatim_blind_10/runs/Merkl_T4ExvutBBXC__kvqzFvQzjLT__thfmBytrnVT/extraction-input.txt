Validation of campaign minimum reward amount on gross value allows creation of sub-threshold campaigns

## Finding description and impact

The `DistributionCreator` contract enforces a minimum reward amount (`rewardTokenMinAmounts`) for whitelisted tokens to ensure campaigns meet a specific economic threshold. This validation is intended to prevent spam and ensure that campaigns are viable for the off-chain reward computation engine.

However, the validation logic in `_createCampaign` checks the **gross** amount provided by the user *before* protocol fees are calculated and deducted.

https://github.com/code-423n4/2025-11-merkl/blob/main/contracts/DistributionCreator.sol#L533

Immediately after this check passes, the contract calculates the fees and updates the campaign amount to the **net** value:

https://github.com/code-423n4/2025-11-merkl/blob/main/contracts/DistributionCreator.sol#L535-L538

If a user creates a campaign with an amount exactly equal to (or slightly above) the minimum, the subsequent fee deduction will drop the final distributed amount **below** the mandatory threshold.

**Impact:**
This discrepancy allows for the creation of "Zombie Campaigns"—campaigns that are valid on-chain but violate the protocol's minimum economic constraints off-chain.
1.  **Fund Lock:** The campaign is successfully created, and the net funds are transferred to the `Distributor` contract.
2.  **Service Denial:** The Merkl off-chain engine, which reads chain state to compute rewards, enforces the `rewardTokenMinAmount` logic to filter out spam or dust campaigns. Because the stored campaign amount is now below this threshold, the engine will likely ignore or reject the campaign.
3.  **Loss of Assets:** Since the engine ignores the campaign, no Merkle root updates will be generated for it. The user's funds remain stuck in the `Distributor` contract indefinitely, indistinguishable from other idle funds, unless manual governance intervention occurs.
