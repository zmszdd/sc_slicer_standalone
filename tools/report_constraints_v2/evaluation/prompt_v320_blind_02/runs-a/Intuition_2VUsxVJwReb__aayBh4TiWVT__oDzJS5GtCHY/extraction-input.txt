End-of-Epoch snapshot logic in `TrustBonding` enables Just-In-Time (JIT) reward theft and systematic yield loss for expiring locks.

## Finding description and impact

### Root Cause
The `TrustBonding` contract calculates a user's pro-rata share of epoch rewards by taking a single point-in-time snapshot of their `veTRUST` (bonded) balance at the absolute end of the epoch. 

The `_userEligibleRewardsForEpoch` function relies on `userBondedBalanceAtEpochEnd`:

Because `ve` tokens (Voting Escrow) are designed to **decay linearly over time**, a point-in-time snapshot creates a critical mismatch between the continuous nature of the lock and the discrete nature of reward distribution.

### Impact 1: Just-In-Time (JIT) Reward Theft
An attacker can wait until the very last block (or second) of a 14-day epoch and call `create_lock()` with a large amount of `TRUST`. Because the snapshot is taken at the end of the epoch, the attacker's balance is "fresh" and has not suffered any decay. 

Meanwhile, honest users who locked at the beginning of the epoch have had their balances decaying for 14 days. As a result, the attacker captures a disproportionately large share of the rewards despite their capital only being at risk for a single second of the 14-day period. This allows attackers to systematically drain the protocol's inflationary emissions, diluting the yield for honest participants to negligible levels.

### Impact 2: Systematic Yield Loss for Expiring Locks
The `VotingEscrow` logic (inherited from Curve) strictly rounds lock expiries down to the nearest `WEEK`. In standard configurations where `EPOCH_LENGTH` is a multiple of weeks (e.g., 14 days), a user's lock will frequently expire exactly at an `epochTimestampEnd`.

If a user is locked for the entire duration of an epoch, but their lock expires at the exact second the epoch ends, `_balanceOf(account, _epochTimestampEnd(epoch))` will evaluate to **0**. The user is then told they have `NoRewardsToClaim`, effectively stealing 14 days of yield that their locked capital earned.
