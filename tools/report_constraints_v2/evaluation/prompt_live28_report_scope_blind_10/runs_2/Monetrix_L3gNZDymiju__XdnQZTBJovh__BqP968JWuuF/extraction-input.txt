`sUSDM` Reports 6 decimals While ERC-4626 Share Accounting Uses 12-decimal Units, causing  Mispricing.

### Description

`sUSDM` sets `_decimalsOffset()` to `6`:

OpenZeppelin's ERC-4626 math uses that offset directly in both share and asset conversions:
With a 6-decimal underlying asset, that means the vault mints share balances in 12-decimal raw units. For example, the first `1e6` USDM deposit mints `1e12` raw share units. However, `sUSDM.decimals()` is overridden to return `6` instead of the `12` decimals implied by the ERC-4626 offset.

So the contract's accounting and its ERC-20 metadata mismatch by `1e6`.




### Impact

Any wallet, bridge, AMM, lending market, or accounting system that normalizes `sUSDM` using `decimals()` will treat balances and supplies as `1e6` times larger than they really are.

Concrete failures include:
1. A user depositing `1 USDM` receives `1e12` raw shares, but every standards-compliant integrator will display that as `1,000,000 sUSDM` instead of `1 sUSDM`.
2. Off-chain systems that derive price-per-share or TVL from `totalSupply()` and `decimals()` will misprice `sUSDM`.
3. If `sUSDM` is ever listed in another DeFi protocol, the wrong decimals can lead to catastrophic collateral or swap normalization mistakes.

Because `sUSDM` is explicitly presented as an ERC-4626 wrapper, this metadata mismatch breaks the assumptions external integrations rely on to handle vault shares safely.




### Recommendation

Make the share token's reported decimals consistent with the ERC-4626 math by removing the custom `decimals()` override and inherit the OpenZeppelin default (`asset decimals + _decimalsOffset()`, which is `12`.)

The share accounting unit and the published ERC-20 decimals must match.
