Operator Griefing via Fund Withdrawal

## Finding description and impact

When an operator (`msg.sender`) creates a campaign on behalf of a creator, the `_pullTokens` function is designed to first use the creator's pre-deposited balance. However, if the creator's balance is insufficient, the logic falls back to pulling the entire campaign amount directly from the operator's wallet via `safeTransferFrom`.

A malicious creator can exploit this by front-running the operator's transaction. They can deposit funds and grant an allowance to lure the operator, then withdraw their funds just before the operator's `createCampaign` call is executed. This ensures the initial balance check fails, triggering the fallback and causing the unsuspecting operator to fund the campaign.


## Attack Scenario

### Step-by-Step Griefing Attack

**Actors:**

- **Alice (Creator):** The malicious attacker.
- **Bob (Operator):** The victim.

1.  **The Lure:**

    - Alice deposits 1000 USDC into the `DistributionCreator` contract using `increaseTokenBalance`. Her internal balance is now 1000 USDC.
    - Alice authorizes Bob as an operator using `toggleCampaignOperator`.
    - Alice grants Bob an allowance of 1000 USDC using `increaseTokenAllowance`.
    - Alice asks Bob to create a 1000 USDC campaign on her behalf.

2.  **The Trap:**

    - Bob prepares and submits a transaction to call `createCampaign`, specifying Alice as the `creator` and a `campaignAmount` of 1000 USDC. He sets a standard gas price.
    - Alice monitors the mempool for Bob's transaction.

3.  **The Front-Run:**

    - Upon seeing Bob's transaction, Alice immediately submits a transaction to call `decreaseTokenBalance` to withdraw her 1000 USDC. She uses a higher gas price to ensure her transaction is mined _before_ Bob's.
    - Alice's withdrawal succeeds. Her internal balance in the contract is now 0.

4.  **The Grief:**
    - Bob's `createCampaign` transaction is now mined.
    - The call proceeds to `_pullTokens`.
    - The check `if (userBalance >= campaignAmount)` becomes `if (0 >= 1000)`, which is **false**.
    - Execution jumps to the final `else` block.
    - The contract calls `IERC20(USDC).safeTransferFrom(msg.sender, ..., 1000)`.
    - Since `msg.sender` is Bob, the 1000 USDC is pulled directly from Bob's wallet (assuming he has given the contract an allowance to spend his USDC, which is a prerequisite for any direct-payment campaign creation).

**Result:** Alice successfully created her 1000 USDC campaign, got all her money back, and forced Bob to pay for the entire thing.

## Impact Assessment

- **Financial Impact:** Direct, unexpected financial loss for campaign operators.
- **System Impact:** The entire operator/delegation feature is rendered untrustworthy and unusable. No rational actor would agree to be an operator under these conditions.
- **Severity Justification:** **HIGH**. While it does not risk protocol funds, it enables a direct theft of funds from a specific and essential user class (operators), breaking a core contract feature.
