public `rebalanceFunds()` allows attacker to repeatedly drain Vault buffer (excess) to Custodian, enabling low-cost griefing/DoS of Fast Access liquidity

**Root cause:**
The FastAccessVault design is asymmetrical: *outgoing* transfers of excess liquidity to the Custodian are executed on-chain and immediately (push), while *incoming* top-ups from the Custodian are handled off-chain and may be slow (event → off-chain processing). The vault exposes a public/external `rebalanceFunds()`-style function that computes the current `excess = vaultBalance - target` and immediately transfers `excess` to the Custodian. There is no caller restriction, no rate-limit/cooldown, no minimum amount threshold, and no upper bound that prevents the Vault from being drained to the target level.

An attacker with a bot can:

1. Monitor Mints (or trigger small mints).
2. Immediately call `rebalanceFunds()` after each mint to push the freshly created `excess` off-chain to the Custodian.
3. Repeat continually (low gas cost per call).

Because incoming replenishment from the Custodian is slow/off-chain, the Vault will be kept at the *minimum target level* (no cushion). If a legitimate large Redeem arrives while the Vault sits at the target, the Vault cannot satisfy fast withdrawals and users must wait for the Custodian to perform an off-chain top-up. In practice this is a low-cost, easily automated griefing/DoS that prevents users from accessing the protocol’s “fast access” liquidity.

**Impact(s):**

* **Denial of Service (DoS) / Griefing:** Fast withdrawals (the entire value proposition of the FastAccessVault) become unavailable to honest users because an attacker can keep the Vault continuously at the bare target.
* **User funds availability risk:** Users who expect immediate/redemptions will be forced to use slow off-chain flows or wait for Custodian top-ups; UX, capital risk and slippage increase.
* **Low cost to attacker:** No privileged access, fully executable by any externally owned account (EOA). Attack cost is gas per call only.
* **Operational burden & reputational damage:** Requires manual intervention, emergency ops or Custodian SLA changes; can degrade trust and produce financial losses (e.g., forced liquidations elsewhere, missed yield opportunities).
* **Composability risk:** Other contracts or strategies relying on the Vault’s “fast” liquidity may break or suffer losses.
