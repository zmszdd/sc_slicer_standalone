HypoVault Share-Inflation

## Summary
`HypoVault` pre-sets `totalSupply = 1 000 000` in the constructor while the vault owns **zero** underlying assets.  When the first deposit is fulfilled, `totalAssets` evaluates to `1`, so shares minted equal


A 1 000-asset deposit therefore mints **1 000 000 000 shares**.  The vault’s share-price collapses (~10⁻⁶ asset per share), permanently corrupting all accounting.

## Root Cause
1. **Bootstrap supply without assets**
2. **Near-zero denominator in fulfilment math**
   Before the first fulfilment `computeNAV() == assetsDeposited`, so `totalAssets == 1`.
3. Both `fulfillDeposits` and `fulfillWithdrawals` use `Math.mulDiv()` with this 1-wei denominator, inflating/devaluing shares and assets.

## Exploit / Impact
* First depositor instantly controls ~100 % of shares for negligible cost.
* Later depositors buy worthless shares; their funds are effectively stolen.
* Withdrawals can burn huge numbers of shares for tiny (or zero) assets.
* Performance-fee logic, NAV reporting and integrations relying on share price are wrong.
* Financial loss is unbounded; all subsequent deposits can be drained.


## Recommended Mitigations
1. **Start with zero supply** – remove the hard-coded `totalSupply` and mint shares only when assets exist.  For the very first deposit, mint `sharesReceived = assetsToFulfill`.
