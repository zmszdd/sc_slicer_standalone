Anyone can withdraw another user’s funds from `WERC7575Vault` once self-allowance is enabled

## Finding description

In the `WERC7575Vault` contract the `withdraw()` and `redeem()` functions allow the caller to specify an arbitrary owner whose shares will be burned and used to withdraw the corresponding assets to another address (likely malicious caller). There exists no check that `msg.sender == owner` or `msg.sender` was approved to withdraw on `owner`'s behalf, instead the vault only calls
`spendSelfAllowance(owner, shares)` itself only checks `allowance[owner][owner]` - the self-allowance, not approval for the attacker. Once a validator signs a permit enabling a user’s self-allowance (required for normal usage), anyone can call `vault.withdraw(assets, attacker, victim)`, burning `victim` user's shares and getting the assets tied to them sent to `attacker`.

## Impact

An attacker can use 100% of the self-allowed shares the victim has to withdraw assets from any `WERC7575Vault`. By using either of the `withdraw()` and `redeem()` functions, the attacker will:
- Burn the victim’s shares
- Withdraw the underlying assets to themselves
- Leave the victim with a zero balance, with no way to recover what was lost.

## Severity Explanation

This enables complete theft of any user’s vault balance by any external attacker, without permission, without cost. No special conditions are needed other than the user having an active self-allowance (which is part of the normal flow of the protocol). The attacker fully drains the user’s position (or as much as they have self-allowed) in a single transaction.
