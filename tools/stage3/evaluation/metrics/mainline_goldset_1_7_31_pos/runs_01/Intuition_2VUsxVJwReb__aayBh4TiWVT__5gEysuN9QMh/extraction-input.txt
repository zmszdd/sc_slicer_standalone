Users Cannot Claim Rewards for the Epoch in Which Their Lock Expires, Causing Guaranteed Loss of Final Epoch Rewards

## Finding description and impact

Every user who lets their lock reach its natural expiry loses 100% of their rewards for the final epoch in which they were bonded. The reward system snapshots `userBondedBalanceAtEpochEnd` at the exact `epochTimestampEnd` timestamp.

As `veTRUST` balance decays linearly to zero at lock expiry, and the lock-end is always <= the epoch-end (due to week-rounding), the snapshot returns *zero* for the *epoch in which the lock expires*. The user was bonded for a part or all of that epoch, yet received nothing.

This affects **100% of locks that reach their natural expiry** without manual extension to unlock time.

For the worst case, users who lock for any duration `<= epochLength` (including the minimum allowed e.g `MINTIME = epochLength = 14 days`) can lose **100% of their rewards**, since the lock can start and expire within the same epoch, leaving no epoch where the end-snapshot captures a non-zero balance.

For example: Consider a user whose lock expires during epoch N 
- **Epoch N-1 rewards**: Captured at `epochTimestampEnd(N-1)`. Lock hasn't expired, balance > 0. Rewards claimable during epoch N. This works fine.
- **Epoch N rewards**: The user was bonded for part or all of epoch N. But the snapshot is at `epochTimestampEnd(N)`, by which point `locked.end <= epochTimestampEnd(N)`, so `_balanceOf` returns **0**. `_userEligibleRewardsForEpoch` returns 0. The user **can never claim** epoch N rewards — they are gone.

### Impact

1. Guaranteed loss of the final epoch's rewards for every expiring lock. This is not probabilistic, any
lock that reaches its `locked.end` loses 100% of rewards for the epoch in which it expires.
2. Up to 100% total reward loss for lockers with duration <= epochLength
3. Contradicts documented design - The NatSpec promises rewards "allocated pro rata to users' shares of the total bonded balance." A user bonded for an entire epoch receiving 0% of their share violates this guarantee.
4. No mitigation without cost - the only escape is extending the lock (more capital lockup than intended), and the user has no way to know they need to do this.

## Recommended mitigation steps

Instead of snapshotting balance at the exact `epochTimestampEnd` (where the linear decay has already zeroed out), the reward calculation should detect when a user's lock expires within the epoch and sample their balance at `locked.end - 1`(the last second they were bonded). 

This captures the user's non-zero balance at the moment just before expiry and gives them proportional credit for the time they were bonded during that epoch.
