DoS of all swap-and-bridge flows because TrustSwapAndBridgeRouter cannot accept ETH from Slipstream's refundETH()

## Description:

`TrustSwapAndBridgeRouter` delegates swaps to the Slipstream (Aerodrome CL) SwapRouter via `exactInput()`. 

After the swap, the Slipstream router calls `refundETH()` (from PeripheryPayments) to return any leftover ETH to the caller:


So the Slipstream router sends its full ETH balance to `msg.sender`, which is `TrustSwapAndBridgeRouter`. `TrustSwapAndBridgeRouter` has no `receive()` or `fallback()` and does not handle incoming ETH. A plain ETH transfer to it reverts. Therefore, if the Slipstream SwapRouter has any ETH balance at the time of the call, the entire `swapAndBridgeWithETH` or `swapAndBridgeWithERC20` transaction reverts.

An attacker can force ETH onto the Slipstream SwapRouter using a contract that calls `selfdestruct(router)`. `selfdestruct()` after EIP-6780 (Cancun) only forwards ETH to the beneficiary without destroying the contract bytecode and does not allow the target to reject ETH; the Slipstream router cannot refuse this. With as little as 1 wei on the Slipstream router, every subsequent `swapAndBridgeWithETH` and `swapAndBridgeWithERC20` call fails. The same selfdestruct contract can be reused (bytecode is no longer removed), so the attack is cheap and repeatable.

## Scenario:

1. Victim prepares a transaction calling `swapAndBridgeWithETH()` or `swapAndBridgeWithERC20()` and broadcasts it to the mempool.

2. Attacker sees the transaction and frontruns it. Attacker deploys a small contract that accepts ETH and has a function that calls `selfdestruct(slipstreamSwapRouter)`.

3. Attacker sends 1 wei to this contract and calls the function. The contract selfdestructs and forces 1 wei to the Slipstream SwapRouter. The router cannot reject this transfer.

4. Victim's transaction is included. It calls `exactInput()` on the Slipstream router. The swap executes, then the Slipstream router calls `refundETH()` and attempts to send its balance (at least 1 wei) to `TrustSwapAndBridgeRouter`.

5. The transfer to `TrustSwapAndBridgeRouter` reverts because the contract has no `receive()` or `fallback()`. The whole transaction reverts and the victim's swap and bridge do not execute.

6. All users are affected: every `swapAndBridgeWithETH` and `swapAndBridgeWithERC20` call fails. The attacker can repeatedly send 1 wei to the Slipstream router using the same contract (EIP-6780 leaves bytecode in place).

## Impact:

Full DoS of swap-and-bridge. Both `swapAndBridgeWithETH` and `swapAndBridgeWithERC20` are unusable whenever the Slipstream SwapRouter holds any ETH. Users cannot swap and bridge from Base to the destination chain via the protocol.

Low cost of attack. Can be repeated many times. One wei per attack (+gas); the selfdestruct contract can be reused. No need to hold or lock significant funds.
