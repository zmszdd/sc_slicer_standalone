Performance fee can be bypassed via manipulated `userBasis`

## Summary

Performance fee is charged for the user with a positive PNL in the vault. PNL is calculated by withdrawal amount redeemed by share balance minus principal represented by `userBasis`.


However, `userBasis` can be changed after user deposits through transfers. `transfer()` and `transferFrom()` allow users to transfer `balance` and `userBasis` proportionally at the same time.

Between different users, multiple transfers can make `balance` unchanged (representing the same withdrawable amount despite fees) while changing `userBasis` (representing the principal which should have been constant). Then the winning user can fabricate a non-positive PNL to bypass the performance fee.

## Finding Description

HypoVault shares are ERC-20 tokens, which implement transfer functions. In `HypoVault::transfer()`:


We can see `userBasis` is transferred with user balance. The two variables `userBasis[user]` and `balanceOf[user]` are designed to follow such an invariant:

$$
\forall \text{user} \in \text{Users}: \begin{cases}
\text{balanceOf}[\text{user}] = 0 \iff \text{userBasis}[\text{user}] = 0 \\
\text{balanceOf}[\text{user}] > 0 \implies \frac{\text{balanceOf}[\text{user}]}{\text{userBasis}[\text{user}]} = k \text{ (constant)}
\end{cases}
$$

However, the following scenario shows how the constant `k` ratio can be broken:

1. `s` represents shares (balanceOf[user]). We suppose share and funds is 1:1.
2. `p` represents principal (userBasis[user]).
3. Next we will suppose the initial states of Alice (loser) and Bob (winner).
4. Alice: (10s, 12p). Bob: (20s, 18p).
5. Bob has made a profit, so he wants to exit now. But if he exits immediately, he must pay performance fee with a positive `PNL = 20 - 18 = 2`. So he decides to make a trade with Alice, who loses money at the moment and does not want to exit.
6. Bob persuades Alice to transfer `10s` to him first.
7. Alice: (0s, 0p). Bob: (30s, 30p).
8. Bob transfers back `10s` to Alice.
9. Alice: (10s, 10p). Bob: (20s, 20p).
10. Alice and Bob have no loss in shares after the transfers. Alice loses money but has a 0 PNL. Bob wins money but also has a 0 PNL.
11. If Bob exits now, he will not have to pay a performance fee. If Alice exits now, she also will not pay a performance fee, and neither would she have before. So Alice never suffers a loss regarding fees, and the trade with Bob does no harm. If Alice decides to stay in the vault, when she makes a profit, she can also do the same trade with others.

(The scenario above is also shown in the POC section.)

The performance fee follows a non-positive-then-zero mechanism, which means the vault operates as a non-zero-sum game. If more users with negative PNL are willing to exit, and more users with positive PNL make trades with them (winners bypass fees and losers get compensated somehow), then it will cause more harm to the protocol's interests. After all, it is unfair to allow any bypass of fees.

## Recommendation

Consider enforcing full transfer or redesigning the balance-basis relationship and performance fee calculation.
