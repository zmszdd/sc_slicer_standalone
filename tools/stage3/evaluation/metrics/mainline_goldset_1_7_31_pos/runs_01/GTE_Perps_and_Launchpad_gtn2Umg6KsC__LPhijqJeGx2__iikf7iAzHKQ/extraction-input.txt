Missing protocol state checks allow deposit and withdraw operations when protocol is inactive

## Description:

The protocol implements an emergency pause mechanism through the `onlyActiveProtocol` modifier that checks `clearingHouse.active` status before allowing critical operations. However, this protection is inconsistently applied across the codebase. While trading functions like `placeOrder`, `liquidate`, and `backstopLiquidate` properly use the `onlyActiveProtocol` modifier, all deposit and withdrawal functions lack this essential safety check.

The affected functions include:
- `deposit(address account, uint256 amount)` in PerpManager
- `withdraw(address account, uint256 amount)` in PerpManager  
- `depositTo(address account, uint256 amount)` in PerpManager
- `depositFromSpot(address account, uint256 amount)` in PerpManager
- `withdrawToSpot(address account, uint256 amount)` in PerpManager
- `deposit(address account, address token, uint256 amount)` in AccountManager
- `withdraw(address account, address token, uint256 amount)` in AccountManager

This means users can continue moving funds in and out of the protocol even when administrators have intentionally paused it due to security concerns, bugs, or other critical issues.

## Attack path:

1. **Protocol vulnerability discovered**: A critical security issue is identified in the protocol

2. **Admin attempts emergency pause**: Protocol administrators call `deactivateProtocol()` to pause trading operations and prevent further damage

3. **Trading functions correctly blocked**: All trading operations (`placeOrder`, `liquidate`, etc.) properly revert with `ProtocolNotActive` error

4. **Deposit/withdraw operations continue**: Malicious actors or uninformed users can still:
   - Deposit funds into a compromised protocol via `deposit()` or `depositTo()`
   - Withdraw funds during the pause via `withdraw()` or `withdrawToSpot()`
   - Move funds between spot and perp accounts via `depositFromSpot()`

5. **State inconsistency and damage amplification**: The continued movement of funds during the emergency pause can:
   - Worsen the impact of the original vulnerability
   - Create inconsistent protocol state that complicates recovery efforts

## Impact:

- **Ineffective emergency response**: The protocol's emergency pause mechanism fails to provide complete protection, undermining its primary safety purpose
- **Increased vulnerability window**: Attackers have additional time to exploit vulnerabilities through fund movements even after trading is paused
- **User fund risk**: New deposits can be lost if deposited into a protocol with known critical issues
