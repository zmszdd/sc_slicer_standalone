Backstop orders posted beyond divergence limits enables denial of backstop liquidations

These functions ensure that orders cannot be *filled* beyond the divergence cap. However, the protocol lacks checks during order *posting*, allowing users to submit orders with prices far outside these bounds. Specifically:

- In `_matchIncomingBid`: The loop breaks if `bestAsk > maxAsk`.
- In `_matchIncomingAsk`: The loop breaks if `bestBid < minBid`.

Consider a scenario involving ETH trading:

1. Bob opens a short position on ETH that becomes undercollateralized due to market movements, making it eligible for backstop liquidation.
2. Alice submits a sell (ask) order at a price significantly above the max diverging ask price.
3. Sdmin or backstop liquidator calls `backstopLiquidate`.
4. The function reverts with a `ZeroOrder()` due to the divergence check.

Executing this requires careful timing: No opposing long orders can exist in the book, as Alice's post-only order would fill against them and revert with `PostOnlyOrderWouldBeFilled()`. This makes it mostly probable on freshly created assets, where orders are scarce.

When malicious user post it, it sit indefinitely on orderbook, because no other user can fill it. The only sensible way to clear it is `delistClose` which isn't a perfect just to clear one order.

On a standard orderbook, pulling this off is less likely due to expensiveness. An attacker would need huge amount of fund to clear GTC or MOC longs before posting the malicious short.

### Alternative impact
A more straightforward and probable bug comes from admin actions. If the divergence cap is reduced via `setDivergenceCap` to a value lower than current orders' prices, previously valid orders could suddenly fall outside the new range. This instantly triggers blocking liquidations across both backstop and standard orderbooks without requiring active exploitation.

## Recommended mitigation steps
Consider, only placing orders when price of order isn't falling outside divergence ranges.

For alternative impact, the most plausible remediation is to forcefully close orders - on both standard and backstop orderbook - falling outside price ranges.
