# Vault unregistration can be blocked by anyone

https://github.com/code-423n4/2025-11-sukukfi/blob/main/src/ShareTokenUpgradeable.sol#L318-320
https://github.com/code-423n4/2025-11-sukukfi/blob/main/src/WERC7575ShareToken.sol#L274-276

## Finding description and impact

Both EIP-7575 share tokens in the code base have the notion of registered vaults that can be set by the owner. 
The requirement that the vaults have absolutely no assets is too strict, on its own; there are many ways for the asset balance to not be zero:

- A malicious user could straight up donate to the contract
- Dust from conversions between assets and shares can accumulate from normal use
- A user, malicious or not, could mint shares and never claim them.

Should a vulnerability or other issue arise with one of the vaults, then this prevents its removal or replacement, because only one vault per asset is permitted. In the case of the investment ("W-") vaults, that situation is permanent; for the others, we'd have to upgrade at least the vault contract, and possibly the share token contract as well, just to deal with this situation.

Furthermore, the limit of 10 asset/vault pairs could lead to the protocol being unable to accommodate new assets, where it could otherwise have done so by retiring other assets.

## Recommended mitigation steps

Allow an administrator to retire vaults even when balances are zero; if the vault is totally malicious, then any assets have to be considered lost anyway.

Consider implementing a way for an administrator to take all assets, and distribute them amongst shareholders somehow. The simplest option would be a redeem-only vault, that is potentially allowed to hold the same asset as active vaults. The administrators could then have the option to transfer assets to that vault, and that vault only.
