Expired Orders Cause Stale Price Discovery in getTOB() Leading to Griefing Attacks and Market Data Corruption

## Finding description and impact


### Root Cause Analysis

The vulnerability stems from incomplete cleanup of expired orders in the Red-Black Tree price level management system, directly affecting the critical getTOB() function that external systems rely on for market data:

- **Incomplete Price Level Removal:** In Book.sol::_updateLimitRemoveOrder(), price levels are only removed when numOrders == 1, meaning only when removing the very last order at that price level.
- **Stale Price Level Persistence:** When expired orders are removed but other orders remain at the same price level, the price level stays in the tree even if all remaining orders are also expired.
- **Unfiltered External Price Discovery:** The getTOB() function returns stale prices from expired orders to external systems without any expiration filtering.

### Attack Scenario:
Malicious actors can:

Place multiple orders at strategic price levels
Allow orders to expire while keeping price levels "alive"
Force the system to maintain many stale price levels
Degrade system performance and increase gas costs for legitimate users

### Impact:
- **External View Functions:**
    - `getTOB()` - Returns potentially stale best bid/ask prices
    - `getOrdersPaginated()` - May start iteration from expired order heads
- **Internal Price Discovery:**
    - `getBestAskPrice()` / `getBestBidPrice()` - No expiration filtering
    - Matching engine functions encounter extra cleanup iterations
- **Tree Management:**
    - `_updateLimitRemoveOrder()` - Incomplete price level cleanup
    - Red-Black Tree maintains stale price levels
